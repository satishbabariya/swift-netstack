# Code of conduct

## The standard

Be respectful. Assume the other person is trying to get something right.

This project cares a great deal about whether claims are true, which means
review here is direct: pull requests get asked "how do you know?" and "what did
you measure?", and comments will point out when an assertion does not hold. That
is aimed at the work, never at the person doing it, and the same questions are
asked of the maintainers' own changes — several commits in this history record
the author's own mistakes in detail.

If you cannot tell whether a comment is about your work or about you, say so and
it will be rephrased.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, in the repository or anywhere the
  project is being discussed.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption: derailing threads, reopening settled decisions without
  new information, or demanding unpaid attention.
- Dismissing someone's report or contribution on the basis of who they are
  rather than what it says.

## Scope

Issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and anywhere someone is
representing this project.

## Reporting

Report privately to the repository owner
([@satishbabariya](https://github.com/satishbabariya)) through GitHub. Reports
are read by the maintainers, kept confidential, and answered.

If your report is about a maintainer, say so; GitHub's own
[abuse reporting](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse) exists for when that
is not enough, and using it is always legitimate.

## What happens next

Depending on what happened: a private word, a public correction, editing or
removing the content, or a block from the repository. Whoever is reported gets
to hear what was reported and to respond, unless doing so would put the reporter
at risk.

Security vulnerabilities are a different process — see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).
